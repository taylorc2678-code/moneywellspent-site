Dorm Items Worth Buying: Bedroom Additions

By Money Well Spent • College Life Guides • 7 min read

"Moving into a dorm can feel sterile and cramped. Having tested these bedroom additions throughout my college experience, here are the exact items that make your space feel homey, stay organized, and solve everyday dorm room struggles."

1. Fairy Lights / Vines

Cozy Ambiance

Having fairy lights around your bed can make your dorm feel much more homey while also giving you a convenient way to turn off the lights without getting out of bed. They’re perfect for those nights when you’re already comfortable under the covers and ready to go to sleep. Instead of getting up, you can simply grab the remote and turn them off. They’re an inexpensive way to add a little extra comfort and personality to your bedroom.

Pros:
  • Makes the room feel more homey
  • Adds a cozy atmosphere
  • Can be turned off from bed with remote
  • Affordable
  • Adds personality to your space
Cons:

Setting them up can take quite a bit of time when you first move in.

2. Bedside Table Tray

Nightstand Alternative

Many dorms have a desk or nightstand near the bed, but they aren’t always at a comfortable height or within easy reach. Some rooms may not have a nightstand at all. A bedside table tray gives you a convenient place to keep things like your water bottle, phone, alarm clock, chapstick, or anything else you might want nearby while relaxing or waking up during the night.

Just keep in mind that some trays may not support the weight of a heavy metal water bottle, depending on how they attach to the bed. It’s also important to install the tray correctly. If it falls, everything sitting on it is going down with it—and cleaning up that mess is not fun.

Pros:
  • Keeps important items within reach
  • Great for rooms without a nightstand
  • Helps keep your bed area organized
  • Convenient for nighttime and mornings
Cons:
  • Depending on type it may not support very heavy items
  • Can fall if it isn’t installed properly

3. Step Stool

Raised Bed Essential

Dorm beds are often raised to create extra storage space underneath. Even if your bed isn’t raised all the way, you may still want to lift it enough to fit storage bins or a dresser underneath. The downside is that getting into your bed can become quite the challenge.

A small step stool makes getting into a raised bed much easier. While your roommates are attempting to launch themselves onto their beds, you can simply step up and get in without the hassle.

Disclaimer: Keep in mind the height of your bed compared to the height of the stool before purchasing to make sure you choose the right size for your dorm.
Pros:
  • Makes getting into a raised bed easier
  • Useful for reaching high storage
  • Small and easy to store
Cons:

A stool can become a tripping hazard in a smaller bedroom. It may be worth sliding it under your bed when you aren’t using it so you don’t accidentally stub your toe.

4. Extension Cord / Power Strip

Absolute Must-Have

This is a must-have for your dorm. There are only so many outlets in a room, and if you have a roommate, you may have to share them. Between your computer, calculator, phone, earbuds, watch, lamp, pencil sharpener, fairy lights, and other dorm essentials, it’s surprisingly easy to run out of places to plug things in.

I recommend getting a power strip with plenty of outlets from the beginning. If you buy one that isn’t large enough, you may be tempted to plug another power strip into it, which can overload the circuit and create a safety hazard. Just make sure the type you buy is allowed by your dorm's housing rules.

Pros:
  • Gives you plenty of extra outlets
  • Useful for all electronics
  • Helps keep cords organized
  • A non-negotiable dorm essential
Cons:

Takes up some space, and you need to be careful not to overload it.

5. Sticky Tack

Reusable Wall Mounting

Sticky tack is one of those little things that can be surprisingly useful in a dorm. Many dorms have restrictions on using tape, nails, or pins to hang pictures and decorations, so sticky tack can give you another way to decorate your walls without making holes.

However, be careful when removing it. Even though it is generally safer for walls than tape or nails, pulling it off too quickly or forcefully can still remove some paint. I like this one best because it always does a good job—don’t throw it out afterwards because it's reusable! I’ve been using mine for 2 years now.

Pros:
  • Useful for hanging decorations
  • Doesn’t require holes in the wall
  • Easy to move around and completely reusable
  • Helpful when decorating dorm walls
Cons:

It can still damage or remove paint if you aren’t careful when taking it off.

6. Room Clock

Distraction-Free Timekeeping

Having a clock in your room isn’t a necessity, but it’s a nice convenience. Instead of stopping what you’re doing to pick up your phone and check the time, you can simply glance at the clock. This is especially useful when you’re rushing to get ready for class or work and need to keep track of the time without getting distracted by your phone.

Advice when choosing: Look for a clock that plugs into the wall so you don’t have to worry about constantly replacing batteries. It’s also helpful to choose one with a built-in alarm in case you need to use it as a backup alarm clock.

The clock shouldn’t be overly large, but the numbers should be easy to read from most, if not all, areas of your room. Make sure the numbers contrast well with the background. For example, red numbers on a black background are easy to see from a distance, so you’re less likely to mistake what time it is.

Pros:
  • Easy to check at a glance
  • Helpful when getting ready in a hurry
  • Keeps you from constantly checking your phone
  • Adds a nice homey touch to your room
Cons:

It isn’t a strict necessity since you already have a clock on your phone.

7. Storage Bins

Space Saver

Extra storage can make a huge difference in a dorm room. The storage space you’re given may not always be enough for everything you brought with you, and an extra three-tier storage bin can help prevent overflowing drawers and cluttered desks.

If your bed is raised, you can slide storage bins underneath it. A three-tier bin can also sit on top of or next to your dresser and essentially give you an extension of your existing storage space. They’re useful for everything from small items and knickknacks to things you would rather keep somewhere more private, such as snacks, personal items, or other belongings you don’t want left in a shared space.

Pros:
  • Adds critical extra storage space
  • Helps reduce desk and closet clutter
  • Can fit under raised beds or next to dressers
  • Useful for keeping personal items organized & private
  • Wheels make moving bins much easier
Cons:
  • Extra bins take up floor space if not under a bed
  • Adding too much heavy weight can break plastic
  • Can be difficult to move without wheels (and wheeled ones are more expensive)
